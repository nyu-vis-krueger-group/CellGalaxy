Additional dataset context:
The dataset consists of lung cancer tissue regions, where malignant epithelial tumour cells coexist with a diverse and spatially heterogeneous tumour microenvironment. Common components include stromal fibroblasts, endothelial structures, and multiple myeloid and lymphoid immune populations such as monocytes/macrophages, neutrophils, dendritic cells, T cells, NK cells, and B-lineage infiltrates. Lung tumours frequently show variable immune activity, ranging from immune-hot regions with active lymphoid aggregates to immune-cold tumour nests with minimal infiltration. Myeloid-rich niches, including monocyte- and neutrophil-dominated areas, may contribute to tissue remodelling or immunosuppressive states, whereas lymphoid-enriched regions can reflect anti-tumour responses or tertiary lymphoid structures. When interpreting clusters, consider the coexistence of multiple lineages, spatial gradients of inflammation, and niche-specific tumour–immune interactions.
