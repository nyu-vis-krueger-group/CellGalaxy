Data background (read before answering):
The project analyzes highly multiplexed spatial proteomics data acquired using CyCIF (≈20–30 protein markers) from human colorectal cancer tissue sections. Whole-slide images are processed with established segmentation pipelines to generate single-cell data, where each cell is associated with a segmentation mask, spatial coordinates, and a unique identifier.

For each segmented cell, per-cell marker intensities are computed using compartment-specific masks (e.g., nuclear, cytoplasmic, or membrane regions). These single-cell feature tables form the primary basis for clustering, visualization, and downstream analysis.

In addition to marker intensity profiles, the dataset is informed by morphology-aware analysis developed in the MORPHAEUS framework, which demonstrates that cellular phenotypes may also reflect shape, spatial organization of signals, and local tissue context. However, cluster interpretation here should be grounded primarily in relative marker enrichment patterns at the single-cell level, rather than pixel-level or patch-based representations.

Low-dimensional embeddings (e.g., UMAP) are used for visualization and clustering but do not provide a direct or linear mapping to individual marker channels. Consequently, cluster interpretation should rely on within-level comparisons of marker behavior, cluster size and prevalence, and spatial consistency with known tissue structures, while remaining cautious about inferring precise functional states.

Additional dataset context:
The dataset consists of human colorectal cancer (CRC) tissue regions acquired with CyCIF, where malignant epithelial tumour cells coexist with a diverse and spatially heterogeneous tumour microenvironment. Common components include stromal fibroblasts, endothelial structures, and multiple myeloid and lymphoid immune populations such as monocytes/macrophages, neutrophils, dendritic cells, T cells, NK cells, and B-lineage infiltrates. CRC tissues frequently exhibit marked spatial variation in immune activity, ranging from immune-enriched regions (e.g., stromal/immune interface or lymphoid aggregates) to immune-excluded tumour glands or nests with limited infiltration. Myeloid-rich niches may contribute to immunosuppressive signalling and tissue remodelling, whereas lymphoid-enriched regions can reflect anti-tumour responses or tertiary lymphoid structure-like organization. When interpreting clusters, consider the coexistence of multiple lineages, spatial gradients across tumour–stroma boundaries, and niche-specific tumour–immune interactions.

Cell states are inferred from segmentation-based single-cell measurements and should be interpreted with awareness of potential spatial spillover and neighborhood influence, as discussed in morphology-aware spatial profiling studies.
